# How to run a playtest

Written for someone who has never done this. If you already run games, skip to
[the five tests](tests.md).

## What a playtest actually is

You sit a few people down, play the game for an hour, and **watch what goes wrong**. That's it.
You are not trying to give them a good time &mdash; a good time is a nice side effect. You are
trying to find the places where the game confuses people, so those places can be fixed before
strangers meet it at a convention.

The hard part is not running the game. The hard part is **writing down what happened** instead
of remembering it fondly afterwards.

??? note "A few words that come up"

    **Narrator** &mdash; the one person who describes the world and decides how hard things are.
    Other games call this the GM or DM. In Does Play Dice they're the Narrator.

    **The Party** &mdash; everyone else. They each play one character.

    **Pregens** &mdash; short for pre-generated characters. Ready-made characters so nobody has
    to invent one before playing. Six of them are in the kit.

    **One-shot** &mdash; a game that starts and finishes in a single sitting, rather than
    continuing week to week.

    **Session** &mdash; one sitting. Usually one to three hours.

---

## Before: about ten minutes

**1. Print what you need.** Everything is on the [Download](../downloads.md) page.

| Print | How many | On what |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Table tent | 1 | Card stock, folded |
| Six characters | 1 sheet, cut up | Card stock |
| Session brief | 1 per session | Plain paper |
| Feedback slips | 1 sheet per 8 players | Plain paper |
| One-shot frame | 1 per session | Plain paper |

**2. Pick tonight's test.** One, not five. Go to [the five tests](tests.md), start at number one,
and only move on once it's answered. Tick it on the session brief.

**3. Fill in the top of the one-shot frame.** The premise, the three ways past the obstacle, the
difficulty for each. Ten minutes is plenty. It's meant to be thin &mdash; you improvise the rest.

**4. Bring dice.** Five per player if you have them: 4, 6, 8, 12 and 20-sided.

---

## During: the actual session

**5. Put the table tent up where everyone can see it.** Don't explain the rules first. Let them
read it. Whether they can is literally test number one.

**6. Hand out the characters.** One each. Let them pick.

**7. Start a stopwatch on your phone.** You need one number tonight: **how long until somebody
rolls a die.** Note the time and put the phone down.

**8. Describe the opening scene and ask what they do.**

**9. Only call for a roll when failing would be interesting.** If someone wants to open an
unlocked door, just tell them what's inside. Rolling for everything is the most common way to
make a light game feel heavy.

**10. Write things down as they happen.** Two things, in the moment:

- Every time somebody asks a rules question, make a mark. Just a tally.
- When a question comes up twice, **write it down word for word.** The exact wording is usually
  the fix.

!!! tip "The thing nobody does, that matters most"

    Take notes **at the table, while it's happening.** Not in the car afterwards.

    An hour later you will remember that it went well. You will not remember that three people
    hesitated at the same sentence on page one &mdash; and that hesitation is the entire value
    of the evening.

---

## After: about five minutes, before anyone leaves

**11. Hand out a feedback slip to each player.** Three questions, thirty seconds. Ask them to be
blunt. Say out loud that unhelpful praise is worse than useless &mdash; people need permission
to criticise something you made.

**12. Fill in the bottom of the session brief.** Five things:

- Time to first roll
- How many rules questions
- Did they finish, or fizzle
- Would they play again &mdash; **ask out loud, and watch the pause before the answer**
- The one thing you'd cut

**13. Photograph everything before you leave.** The session brief and all the slips.

!!! danger "This is the step that gets skipped"

    Paper that goes into a bag stays in the bag. A photograph takes ten seconds and it is the
    only thing that turns an evening into data.

**14. Add a row to the [progress log](progress.md).**

---

## How often

Aim for **two a week** &mdash; the Tuesday library meetup and the larger Wednesday one. That's
roughly ten sessions before the convention.

They are the scarcest resource in the whole plan. A missed week costs two of them, and there is
no way to buy them back.
