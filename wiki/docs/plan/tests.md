# The five tests

Each session answers **one** question. Work down the list in order &mdash; each one assumes the
previous is settled.

!!! info "Why one question per session"

    A session where you're watching for five things is a session where you notice none of them.
    Pick one, watch it closely, write it down, move on when it's answered.

---

## Can a stranger start playing in 15 minutes?

**Run it as:** hand them the table tent and the characters. Say almost nothing. Start the timer.

**Write down:** minutes to the first dice roll. Where they stalled. Which sentence somebody
read twice.

**Good result:** under 15 minutes, and the questions are about the *story*, not the rules.

**Bad result:** over 20 minutes, or the same sentence trips more than one person. If two
different tables stall in the same place, that sentence is the problem &mdash; not the players.

**Answered when:** two tables in a row get there under 15.

---

## Does the tie rule land?

Ties are the most distinctive thing in the game: **both sides get something.** You get through
the door, and you hurt your shoulder doing it. It's also the easiest thing to fumble, because
the Narrator has to invent the complication on the spot.

**Run it as:** normal play, but set at least two challenges where a tie is likely &mdash; match
the Difficulty die to the players' Skill die.

**Write down:** what the Narrator did on each tie. Did they improvise something good, freeze, or
quietly ignore it and just let the player win?

**Good result:** ties feel like the most interesting outcome, not an awkward one.

**Bad result:** the Narrator freezes, or treats a tie as a failure. That means the rule needs
examples, not rewording.

**Answered when:** a Narrator who isn't Jeff handles a tie cleanly without asking what to do.

---

## Do people use all three Skills?

Every character has Physical, Mental and Social. The design promise is that there's more than one
way past any obstacle.

**Run it as:** use the one-shot frame properly &mdash; write three routes past the obstacle, one
per Skill, *before* you sit down.

**Write down:** which Skill each player reached for, each time. Just tally marks in three columns.

**Good result:** all three get used, and somebody solves something in a way you didn't plan for.

**Bad result:** everything gets solved Physically. That's almost never the players' fault
&mdash; it means the obstacles aren't really offering three routes, and the fix is in how
challenges get written.

**Answered when:** two sessions where all three Skills got used meaningfully.

---

## Do the campaign rules break the one-page feel?

The whole promise of the game is that someone who read one page and someone who read everything
can play at the same table. This is the first real test of that.

**Run it as:** a mixed table. At least one player using only the one-page rules, at least one
using the campaign rules.

**Write down:** did the one-page player ever feel behind, confused, or like they were playing a
lesser version? Ask them directly afterwards.

**Good result:** the one-page player never notices they're missing anything.

**Bad result:** the one-page player waits while the other one does arithmetic. That's the
warning sign, and it means the campaign rules need to be *optional*, not *additive*.

**Answered when:** a mixed table plays a full session and the one-page player says they wouldn't
have known.

!!! note "Only when the campaign rules exist"

    Skip this one until there's a draft to test. Don't stall the programme waiting for it
    &mdash; keep running tests 1 to 3 with new people.

---

## Does a 40-minute convention slot work?

At a convention, people sit down cold, play, and leave. There is no teaching beforehand and no
second session.

**Run it as:** a full dress rehearsal. Timer visible. Pregens only. The real printed kit. No
introduction beyond what's on the table tent.

**Write down:** did it reach an ending in 40 minutes? Where did it run long? Did anyone leave
early?

**Good result:** a satisfying ending inside 40 minutes, and at least one person asks where to
get the game.

**Bad result:** it runs to 60 minutes, or ends mid-scene. Fix by making the scenario smaller
&mdash; never by rushing the table.

**Answered when:** two timed runs land inside 40 minutes.
