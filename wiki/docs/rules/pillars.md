# The three kinds of play <span class="dpd-status stub">Scaffold, not a proposal</span>

<div class="dpd-protobar" markdown>

one sets out the shape and asks the questions, because Jeff's notes here are genuinely
unresolved &mdash; and in one place he collected the argument *against* his own instinct, which
is worth more than a recommendation from me.

**Confidence: low**, and honestly so. Sources: `Pillars - Tactical.md`,
`Pillars - Social.md`, `Pillars - Discovery.md`.
</div>

## What the three pillars are

Most tabletop games sort play into three modes: **fighting, talking, and exploring.** Jeff's
notes use them as an organising idea and name them inconsistently &mdash; Tactical / Social /
Discovery in the filenames, Combat / Exploration / Social in `Playing the Game.md`.

They are not the same as the three **Skills**. This is worth stating plainly because the words
overlap and it has caused confusion already:

| | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Skills** &mdash; Physical, Mental, Social | Three numbers on your sheet. What your character is like |
| **Pillars** &mdash; fighting, talking, exploring | Three kinds of scene. What the table is doing right now |

A fight can be won with a Social roll. A negotiation can turn on a Physical one. **The pillar
does not pick the Skill** &mdash; the approach does, which is exactly what the one page already
says.

## Fighting

Covered in [Combat](combat.md). This is the only pillar where his notes reach a design.

## Talking — and an argument against writing rules for it

Jeff's social notes are mostly other people's material, but the most interesting thing in the
file is that he kept a paragraph making the case *against* the thing a designer instinctively
wants to build:

!!! quote "Collected in `Pillars - Social.md`"

    *"The assumption you're making here is that players WANT social interaction to be more
    gameified and to have a higher buy-in cost. In my experience, they don't... You'll notice
    that very few games are actually released where that is the case. I think designers tend to
    fall in love with the idea of adding new systems and mechanics without stopping to wonder
    if those systems will actually be fun for the people who wind up using them."*

He did not have to keep that. He kept it because it is a real objection to a "social combat"
system, and it deserves an answer before anything is written.

!!! decision "OPEN DECISION — does talking need rules at all?"

    The one page already handles it: describe what you are doing, roll Social against a
    Difficulty die, the Narrator says what happens. A tie means you got *something*.

    **The case for stopping there:** it works, it is already written, and the objection above
    is persuasive.

    **The case for more:** Jeff's Skills give Social equal billing with Physical, and stamina
    makes losing an argument mechanically as real as losing a fight. If a social defeat can put
    you out of a scene, some structure around how that happens seems fair to the player it
    happens to.

    **Question for Jeff:** is the one page enough here? If it is, this section becomes three
    paragraphs of advice and that is a good outcome, not a gap.

## Exploring

The least resolved of the three, and the place where the notes need the most care.

The exploration material in `Pillars - Discovery.md` &mdash; supplies as party hit points, safe
havens, a catalogue of rated Exploration Challenges &mdash; **reads as transcribed from another
game's design writing**, not as Jeff's own draft. It says *"one mechanic we're adding"* and
refers to *"the core rulebook"* as something other than this one. It has not been drawn on, and
it should not be, until its origin is confirmed.

What *is* clearly his is a body of research on travel: how far you can see from a given height,
how far you can walk in a day across different terrain, how much a horse or a heavy load
changes it. That is reference material rather than a rules system, and it is genuinely useful
&mdash; it belongs in a Narrator's appendix.

!!! decision "OPEN DECISION — does travel cost anything?"

    [Getting better](training.md) proposes the smallest possible version: a hard day's travel
    costs a point of Physical stamina, and a night without food does not give it back. No new
    track, no supply units, no inventory.

    **Question for Jeff:** is that enough, or does exploration want a system of its own? And
    separately &mdash; can he confirm where the supplies-and-havens passage came from? If it is
    his, this whole section changes.

## Why this chapter exists at all

Possibly it should not, and that is a legitimate outcome.

If fighting is covered in its own chapter, talking is covered by the one page, and exploring is
a paragraph in another chapter, then "the three pillars" is a way of thinking about play rather
than a part of the rules &mdash; which would make this a page in **For Narrators**, not a rules
chapter.

That is the recommendation, tentatively. **It is easier to argue with a page that admits what
it is not.**
