## A class is not a thing you are

!!! quote "From `Classes and training paths.md`"

    *"Your class is simply a training path. There is no need (or very rare need) to train in
    multiple classes, because everything relevant to your class, even if you're a hybrid class,
    is an option in your own class tree."*

You do not pick a Warrior and become one. **You spend training on martial things, and people
start calling you a warrior.** The label is a description of what you have done, applied
afterwards.

This matters more than it sounds. It means there is no multiclassing rule, because there is no
class to multi. There is only where your points went.

## What a path costs you

Jeff is firm that breadth should not be free:

!!! quote "Same file"

    *"Major abilities should have a barrier to entrance. This prevents someone from dipping a
    toe into a class to get major advantages... A hybrid class should never be as good a fighter
    as a pure fighter. That said, conversely, there should be something they're better at than
    anyone else."*

So: **the deep end of any path requires most of your points to have gone there.** Not a rule
forbidding you &mdash; a cost you cannot avoid. And the person who split their training is not
a worse version of either specialist; they are the only one who can do the thing that sits
between the two.

## The paths

His sketch has two named trees and gestures at a third.

**Martial** &mdash; unarmed, then melee or ranged, then a specific weapon family. Swords,
hammers, bows, thrown.

**Arcane** &mdash; and this one is the find of the whole folder:

| School | Die | Planet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Air | d8 | Mercury |
| Water | d20 | Venus |
| Aether | d12 | Earth |
| Fire | d4 | Mars |
| Earth | d6 | Jupiter |

!!! success "This is the cosmology, working as a mechanic"

    The five schools of magic **are** the five dice, which **are** the five Platonic solids,
    which **are** the five elements — and each carries a planet.

    That is not decoration bolted onto a rules system. It is the reason the setting is called
    Timaeus, after the Plato dialogue that assigns the solids to the elements, and Jeff cites
    Kepler's *Mysterium Cosmographicum* elsewhere in his notes for exactly this. **The dice on
    the table are the cosmology of the world.**

    Very few games get to do this. It is worth building the magic system around rather than
    treating as flavour.

**General** &mdash; everything neither martial nor arcane. Jeff's own lists are usable as-is:
subterfuge, performing arts, medicine, survival, languages, engineering, riding, boating,
swimming.

## Naming what you do

A small thing from his notes, kept because it costs nothing and is worth a great deal:

!!! quote "From `Using Resources.md`"

    *"D&D 4e refers to the spells of a cleric as prayers. I like this. A lot. Wizard: spells.
    Cleric: prayers. Bard: performances."*

Your path names what you produce. Spells, prayers, performances, orders, songs, stories.
Mechanically identical, and it tells everyone at the table who your character is before you
describe anything.

## Where this draft stops

Jeff's file also contains starting dice per class &mdash; Warrior Body d8 / Mind d4 / Soul d6,
and so on. **Those are not proposed here**, for a reason worth flagging:

!!! decision "OPEN DECISION — Body/Mind/Soul, or Physical/Mental/Social?"

    Every class sketch in his notes uses **Body, Mind and Soul**. The frozen one page uses
    **Physical, Mental and Social**. They are not the same three things, and this is the second
    chapter where the difference has surfaced.

    A *Soul* die is faith, will, and inner life. A *Social* die is other people. A hermit with
    enormous Soul and no Social is a coherent character; under the one page they are the same
    number.

    **Question for Jeff:** are these three or four things? It is possible the honest answer is
    four, and that the one page simply could not fit them. That would be a real finding, and it
    would change this chapter, [Harm](harm.md), and the character sheet.

    Until it is settled, no starting dice can be written down without guessing.

Nor does this draft propose a full skill tree. His sketch is two levels deep in one branch and
one level deep in the rest &mdash; enough to see the shape, not enough to infer the whole. That
is authorship, and it is his.
