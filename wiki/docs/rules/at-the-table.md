# At the table <span class="dpd-status stub">Draft</span>

<div class="dpd-protobar" markdown>
**Written for the site, not from a transcript.** The people are invented; the rolls are not.
Every die below is one the <a href="../">Basic Rules</a> would actually call for, and the
results are what they are. Nothing was nudged to make the point.

The rules take ten minutes to read. This takes two, and it is probably the faster way in.

Three people at a table. **Jess** is the Narrator. **Priya** plays Wren, a clockmaker's
apprentice &mdash; Physical d8, Mental d12, Social d4. **Theo** plays Bram, who works the docks
&mdash; Physical d12, Mental d6, Social d8.

They want a ledger out of a print shop that closed three hours ago.

<p class="dpd-say"><b>Theo</b> Bram walks straight up. We're not sneaking past a man who's

<p class="dpd-say dpd-say--nar"><b>Jess</b> That's Social, and he's bored rather than stupid.

## What happened there

**Losing didn't stop anything.** Bram failed his roll and the scene kept going &mdash; it just got
harder, and now a man remembers his face. A loss that ends the evening is a Narrator problem,
not a rules problem.

**The tie is the whole game.** Wren got through the window *and* hurt her shoulder, and thirty
seconds later the shoulder was the scene. Most systems would have given her a yes or a no. This
one gave her a story, and it did it by accident, because two dice landed on five.

**Nobody looked anything up.** Three rolls, three Skills, one number each side. That is the
entire procedure, and it does not get more complicated later.

If you want to feel the tie rather than read about it, the roller on the
<a href="../">Basic Rules</a> page rolls real dice, and the <a href="../odds/">odds page</a> has
how often each pairing lands there &mdash; it is more often than people expect.
