# Combat, harm and recovery

You already know how combat works, because it's the same roll as everything else: **your Skill
die against theirs, highest wins, and a tie means both sides get something.**

What follows only answers the questions a fight raises that a locked door doesn't: how you get
hurt, what happens when you can't go on, and how you come back. For who goes when, see
[Taking turns](taking-actions.md).

---

## Stamina

**Stamina is how much you can take before you're out.** There are no hit points.

You have a stamina track for each Skill: Body, Mind and Soul. It starts equal to the
number of sides on that Skill's die.

When something costs you &mdash; a blow landed, an argument lost, a horror witnessed &mdash;
you take **one point of stamina in the Skill that suffered**. Nothing else happens.

**Losing stamina doesn't change your rolls.** It costs you in only two ways: at zero you're
out, and getting it back takes time (see [Coming back](#coming-back)).

**At zero stamina in any Skill you are out.** Not dead &mdash; out. A fighter with a d20
Body and a d4 Soul is a fighter who can be talked out of the fight in four sentences.

## Attacking

**Both of you roll.** You roll the Skill die that fits how you're fighting; they roll theirs.

- **You win** &mdash; you land it. They lose **one stamina in the Skill they defended with.**
- **They win** &mdash; the exchange goes against you, and *you* lose one stamina. A fight you
  are losing costs you something even on your own turn.
- **A tie** &mdash; both of you get something. You land it *and* they do.

**A hard hit.** Beat their roll by more than their die has sides, and it costs them **two**
stamina instead of one. Beating a d4 by five is a hard hit; beating a d20 by five is just a
hit.

**Every attack after your first steps down.** Your first attack on your turn rolls your full
die. Your second rolls one die smaller, and your third two smaller: a d12 fighter swings d12,
then d8, then d6. Three swings are allowed, but there's usually something better to do with
that third action.

The dice go d20, d12, d8, d6, d4. A step down is the next one smaller, and nothing goes below
a d4.

## Defending

**Defending is free.** It doesn't cost an action, and you always get to roll. An attack never
just lands on someone who sees it coming: even an archer can miss.

**Every defence after your first in a round steps down.** Against the first attack on you each
round, you roll your full die. Against the next, one die smaller, and so on down to a d4. When
the next round starts, you're back to full.

Attacks and defences are counted separately. Swinging three times on your turn doesn't weaken
your defence.

So being outnumbered is a real problem: by the fourth attacker, a d12 fighter is defending on a
d4. The answer isn't another rule. It's the story: back into a doorway, put a table between
you, or leave.

## Leaving

**You can always retreat.** Say you're going, and you go.

The Narrator decides what it costs &mdash; the thing you came for, the ground you held, the
person you were carrying &mdash; but not whether it works. Nobody gets a free swing at your
back. A losing fight is a decision, not a trap.

## Weapons

A weapon doesn't change the roll. It changes **what winning looks like.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Slashing** &mdash; sword, axe | On a win, you may move the hit to a second enemy within reach |
| **Piercing** &mdash; spear, arrow, dagger | They lose one further stamina at the end of the round unless someone tends to it |
| **Crushing** &mdash; hammer, club, staff | Armour does not help them &mdash; see below |

Armour isn't a number. It's already in your Body die: a knight in good plate and a
halfling who is impossible to catch can both be a d12, and each explains it their own way.
**Crushing weapons ignore that explanation** &mdash; against a hammer, roll the die you would
have had without the armour.

## Hitting someone who can't fight back

Not everything is a contest. When they can't meaningfully resist, the Narrator rolls a
Difficulty die instead of a Skill die:

| Situation | Narrator rolls |
| --- | --- |
| Asleep, bound, or unaware | d4 |
| Distracted but able to move | d6 |
| Moving, but not defending against you | d8 |
| Actually defending themselves | their Skill die |

## What being out means

Out isn't a coma and it isn't a countdown. It's **whatever the story says it is**, chosen by the
table from the Skill that broke:

| The Skill that hit zero | What that looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Body** | Unconscious, winded, a leg that won't hold you |
| **Mind** | Overwhelmed, concussed, unable to hold a thought |
| **Soul** | Humiliated, broken, unable to face the room |

You can be carried, tied up, questioned, argued with, or left. You can't act.

The same goes for the people you fight. A defeated enemy is out, not dead: you can tie them up,
haul them somewhere safe, wake them up to question them, or leave them to recover.

**Nobody dies unless the table decides they do.** Death is available &mdash; some stories need
it &mdash; but it's a choice someone makes out loud, not the result of arithmetic.

## Coming back

Stamina returns three ways, and each kind of hurt has its own kind of cure:

| | Recovers | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A moment's respite** | 1 point in one Skill | Catching your breath, a word from a friend |
| **A night's rest** | All Body and Mind | Requires food and shelter |
| **Care from someone** | 1 point, any Skill | Medicine for Body, counsel for Mind, company for Soul |

**Soul stamina doesn't come back on its own overnight.** Someone has to give it to you. A night
alone doesn't fix having been humiliated in front of people who matter.

## Scars

When something ought to leave a mark, it leaves a mark &mdash; written on the character sheet,
not tracked as a penalty.

It doesn't have to be punishing. If the moment doesn't call for a lasting disadvantage, make it
something you can see: a wound, a scar, a burn. A scar is part of who someone is now, and it
should simply be true about you.

## What's not here

No grid, no flanking, no attacks of opportunity, no called shots, no critical-hit deck. No wound
categories, no injury tables, no death saves. Each one would add a table to a game whose whole
promise is that there are none. If your group wants them, they belong in an optional module.
