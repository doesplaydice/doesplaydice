# Combat <span class="dpd-status stub">Draft proposal</span>

<div class="dpd-protobar" markdown>

## Combat is not a separate game

The most important thing this chapter does is refuse to invent a second system. You already
know how combat works, because it is the same roll as everything else: **your Skill die against
theirs, highest wins, a tie means both sides get something.**

What follows only answers the questions a fight raises that a locked door does not.

!!! quote "Jeff's sequence, from `Pillars - Tactical.md`"

    *"Combat is an opposed roll. The attacker, if they hit, does damage equal to the difference
    between the rolls. **Yes, and** &mdash; either combatant can do damage, so if the defender
    wins the opposed roll, they do damage to the attacker. **Yes, and** &mdash; a defender can
    only defend from a set number of attacks based on their particular skill... **Yes, and**
    &mdash; attacks of any type (attack, defense, opportunity attack) all come from the same
    pool."*

    That is the design. It is his, it is coherent, and everything below is built on it.

## Attacking

**Both of you roll.** You roll the Skill die that fits how you are fighting; they roll theirs.

- **You win** &mdash; you land it. They lose **one stamina in the Skill they defended with.**
- **They win** &mdash; the exchange goes against you, and *you* lose one stamina. A fight you
  are losing costs you something even on your own turn.
- **A tie** &mdash; both of you get something. You land it *and* they do.

That last line matters and it is a deliberate correction. One note reads *"A tie is (always) a
push; no winner"* &mdash; which contradicts the frozen one page. **The one page wins.** A tie in
a fight is the moment the game is most itself: you get through the door and you hurt your
shoulder doing it.

!!! decision "OPEN DECISION — how hard is a hard hit?"

    Jeff proposes reading severity from the *margin* rather than from a natural 20:

    *"A crit is defined by the difference in the rolls, not a nat 20. On a d6: win by 1-3: hit.
    Win by 4-5: hard hit. Win by 6: critical hit."*

    This is a genuinely good idea and it fits step dice perfectly &mdash; but a fixed 1-3 / 4-5
    / 6 ladder only works on a d6. Beating a d20 by six is ordinary; beating a d4 by six is
    impossible.

    **This draft proposes the simplest version that scales:** beat them by **more than their
    die has sides** and it costs them **two** stamina instead of one. Nothing else changes,
    and there is no table to consult.

    **Question for Jeff:** is one extra point enough of a spike, or does a clean hit want to
    feel bigger than that?

## Defending

You may defend **as many times per round as you have actions left.** Defence comes out of the
same three actions as everything else &mdash; his rule, and it is the one that makes being
outnumbered frightening without any new mechanic.

**An attack you do not defend against simply lands.** No roll. One stamina, straight off.

So three attackers against one defender is a real problem, and the answer is not a subsystem.
It is the fiction: back into a doorway, put a table between you, or leave.

## Leaving

**You can always retreat.** Say you are going, and you go.

The Narrator decides what it costs &mdash; the thing you came for, the ground you held, the
person you were carrying &mdash; but not whether it works. Nobody gets a free swing at your
back.

!!! note "Why there are no attacks of opportunity"

    Jeff kept a long complaint about them in his notes, and the objection is sound: *"Tell me
    who is easier to hit: a guy standing right in front of you, or a guy moving away from
    you?"*

    He also kept the counter-argument from 13th Age, which he preferred: *"you can almost
    always retreat. You just say you're going to and you do. The GM can pick what it costs
    you."* That is the version here. It means a losing fight is a decision rather than a trap.

## Weapons

A weapon does not change the roll. It changes **what winning looks like.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Slashing** &mdash; sword, axe | On a win, you may move the hit to a second enemy within reach |
| **Piercing** &mdash; spear, arrow, dagger | They lose one further stamina at the end of the round unless someone tends to it |
| **Crushing** &mdash; hammer, club, staff | Armour does not help them &mdash; see below |

Armour is not a number. It is already in your Physical die: a knight in good plate and a
halfling who is impossible to catch can both be a d12, and each explains it their own way.
**Crushing weapons ignore that explanation** &mdash; against a hammer, roll the die you would
have had without the armour.

## Hitting someone who cannot fight back

Not everything is a contest. When they cannot meaningfully resist, the Narrator rolls a
Difficulty die instead of a Skill die, and Jeff's own ladder applies:

| Situation | Narrator rolls |
| --- | --- |
| Asleep, bound, or unaware | d4 |
| Distracted but able to move | d6 |
| Moving, but not defending you | d8 |
| Actually defending themselves | their Skill die |

## What this chapter does not do

**No initiative beyond what [Taking actions](taking-actions.md) already sets out.** No grid, no
flanking rules, no attacks of opportunity, no called shots, no critical-hit deck. All of those
appear in his notes as things he was reading about rather than things he had decided, and every
one of them adds a table to a game whose whole promise is that there are none.

They belong in an optional tactical module for tables that want them &mdash; which is exactly
what the modular structure in `Summary (April 2024).md` is for.
