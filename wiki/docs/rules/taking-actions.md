# Taking actions <span class="dpd-status stub">Draft proposal</span>

<div class="dpd-protobar" markdown>

It was assembled from his own notes &mdash; principally `Taking Actions.md` and
`Summary (April 2024).md` &mdash; by picking one option wherever those notes contain several
and saying why. **Every choice here is meant to be overruled.** Where a decision was close,

## What this chapter is for

The one page answers *how do I find out whether something works.* It does not answer *how
much can I do before someone else gets a turn* &mdash; and that question only shows up once
a fight starts or a scene gets crowded.

That is the entire job of this chapter. It adds nothing to the one page and changes nothing
on it. A table that has read only the one page and a table that has read both are playing
the same game.

!!! quote "Jeff's own constraint, from `Summary (April 2024).md`"

    *"Let's get rid of the need to translate a number to its corresponding bonus or penalty.
    Let's use the whole scale. Let's minimize the math, meaning a target number should be
    achievable by the dice alone."*

    Every proposal below was tested against that sentence. Several of the drafts in his notes
    fail it, and he says so himself in a margin note: *"There's a lot of tedious math here.
    Too much."* Where his notes and his principle disagreed, **the principle won.**

## The turn

**Everyone rolls a die for order. Highest goes first.** Roll the Skill die that fits how you
are entering the scene &mdash; Physical if you are already moving, Mental if you saw it
coming, Social if you are talking your way in.

That is the whole of initiative, and it reuses the dice already in your hand.

On your turn you may **act** or **hold**.

- Your actions may be spent on your turn, or at any point before your next turn.
- Actions may be split. Move, look, then move again.
- Any action you have not spent when your next turn arrives is **lost**. Nothing carries over.

!!! note "This is Jeff's, nearly untouched"

    The act-or-hold structure, the right to spend actions before your next turn, splitting,
    and losing what you do not use are taken from the **Initiative** section of
    `Summary (April 2024).md` almost word for word. It was one of the most finished things
    in the notes. The only addition is *which* die you roll for order, which his notes leave
    open.

## Three actions

**You get three actions on your turn.** Anything you want to do costs at least one.

Speaking costs nothing. Neither does noticing something obvious, or dropping something you
are holding.

!!! decision "OPEN DECISION — three, or as many as your stamina allows?"

    Jeff's notes point two ways and never resolve it.

    `Taking Actions.md` says *"anything a player wants to do is at least one action (a la
    Pathfinder)"* &mdash; and Pathfinder 2e gives three.

    `Summary (April 2024).md` instead says stamina *"determines how many actions the character
    can take, active and defensive separately."*

    **This draft picks three, fixed.** Reasons: it is a number a new player holds without
    looking anything up; it makes "spend two actions to do it harder" a real decision rather
    than an accounting exercise; and it keeps the action count off the character sheet. A
    stamina-driven count means every player recalculates how many actions they have every
    round, which is exactly the arithmetic the core mechanic refuses to do everywhere else.

    **If Jeff prefers the stamina version**, everything below still works &mdash; replace
    "three" with "your current stamina" and nothing else changes.

## Spending more than one

You may spend **two or three actions on a single attempt.** Each extra action past the first
lets you **roll an additional die and keep the best result.**

One action, a d8 Skill against a d12 door: roll one d8. Three actions: roll three d8s and take
the highest.

This is the same machinery as advantage, which the one page already implies and Jeff states
outright &mdash; *"given advantage or disadvantage, they roll twice and take the better or
worse of those rolls. No math."* Effort buys dice, not bonuses.

The Narrator may rule that some things cannot be hurried or repeated. Picking one lock harder
is reasonable. Persuading the same guard three times in ten seconds is not.

## Reacting

You may spend an action **out of turn** to respond to what someone else does.

A reaction resolves **after** the thing that triggered it. It does not interrupt and it does
not undo. If someone swings at you and you react, their swing happens, then yours.

You may also **change an action you have declared but not yet taken**, if the situation moves
underneath you. You do not have to say what you are doing instead until you do it.

!!! note "Also Jeff's"

    Both paragraphs come from the **Reactions** section of `Summary (April 2024).md`. The
    "resolves after, does not interrupt" rule is the load-bearing one: it means a reaction can
    never be used to cancel someone else's turn, which is what makes reactions safe to hand
    out freely.

## Stamina

**Stamina is a ceiling on what you can roll.** It is not hit points and there is nothing to
subtract.

You have a stamina track for each Skill: Physical, Mental and Social. It starts equal to the
number of sides on that Skill's die.

When something costs you &mdash; a blow landed, an argument lost, a horror witnessed &mdash;
you take **one point of stamina in the Skill that suffered**. Nothing else happens.

A stamina penalty caps your roll on that Skill. **Roll higher than your ceiling and the result
becomes your ceiling.**

| Your Physical die | Stamina lost | Ceiling | You roll a 6, you get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| d8 | 0 | 8 | 6 |
| d8 | 2 | 6 | 6 |
| d8 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| d8 | 7 | 1 | 1 |

**At zero stamina in any Skill you are out.** Not dead &mdash; out. A fighter with a d20
Physical and a d4 Social is a fighter who can be talked out of the fight in four sentences.

!!! quote "This is his, and it is the best idea in the notes"

    From `Summary (April 2024).md`: *"Stamina is a ceiling on a character's rolls in the same
    way that training is a floor... If a person's stamina in any trait is reduced to 0, they
    are incapacitated."* And: *"Physical stamina is the ability to run, fight, defend
    yourself. Mental stamina affects clear thinking... Spiritual stamina is purpose, drive,
    or faith, and a failure brings on the proverbial existential crisis."*

    It removes hit points from the game without removing danger, it makes a social or mental
    defeat mechanically as real as a physical one, and it costs a single number per Skill.

!!! decision "OPEN DECISION — Social or Spiritual?"

    The frozen one page says the three Skills are **Physical, Mental, Social**. Most of the
    design notes say **Physical, Mental, Spiritual** (or Body / Mind / Soul).

    This draft follows the one page, because the one page is canon and this chapter is not
    allowed to contradict it.

    But it is not only a word. *Social* stamina running out means you have been out-argued,
    shamed, or worn down by other people. *Spiritual* stamina running out means you have lost
    your nerve or your faith, which does not need anyone else in the room. **They are
    different games**, and the difference shows up the first time a character is alone and
    frightened.

    **Question for Jeff:** was Social a deliberate replacement for Spiritual, or did the
    one-pager just land on the friendlier word?

## What I cut, and why

Four things in the notes were left out on purpose. Each is easy to put back.

**Armour class and damage reduction.** Jeff's own later idea is better and is already in the
notes: *"what if armour is taken into account in the base ability die?"* A character in good
armour simply has a bigger Physical die, and explains it however they like. One fewer number,
one fewer subtraction, and it lets a halfling and a knight arrive at d20 by different stories.

**Hit points as a pool.** Three separate combat drafts compute them by subtracting one roll
from another and comparing against armour and multiples of die values. Stamina does the same
work with a single point and no arithmetic.

**Attacker stamina loss on a miss.** Jeff already crossed this out himself: *"let's get rid of
attacker stamina hits for a one-way attack."*

**Wound categories, injury tables, and the CON-die wound ladder.** Real design, and far too
much of it for a chapter that has to sit next to a one-page game. It belongs in an optional
injuries module for tables that want it &mdash; which is exactly what the modular structure in
`Summary (April 2024).md` is for.

## Still to decide

Beyond the two decisions above, three things this chapter deliberately does not answer:

1. **What one action of movement is.** Feet, metres, squares, or "somewhere sensible". The
   rest of the game measures nothing, so a number here would be the only measured thing in it.
2. **Whether training exists at v1.** Jeff's floor-versus-ceiling idea &mdash; training raises
   your minimum roll, stamina lowers your maximum &mdash; is elegant and pairs perfectly with
   stamina. It is also a whole progression system.
3. **The difficulty ladder's names.** The live one page says Easy / Moderate / Challenging /
   High difficulty / "Impossible". The notes say Easy / Effort / Difficult / Expert /
   Impossible. Same dice, different words. The one page wins by default, but *Effort* is a
   better word than *Moderate* and it would be a shame to lose it.
