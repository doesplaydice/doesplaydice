# Harm, death and recovery <span class="dpd-status stub">Draft proposal</span>

<div class="dpd-protobar" markdown>
**A proposal for Jeff to react to, not canon, and not his writing.** Built from
`Death, dying, resurrection.md`, which contains both his own table experience and his own
conclusion about what death should mean here. **Confidence: high** on the shape,
**medium** on the numbers.

Nothing from `Rules/Examples/` was read.
</div>

## There are no hit points

Jeff worked this out himself, in a margin note after a long argument with himself about
whether hit points are meat or morale:

!!! quote "From `Death, dying, resurrection.md`"

    *"So from a realism perspective, hit points equal stamina. That means, as they go down, a
    character grows exhausted."*

That is the whole system. **Stamina is the only track**, it is the same one described in
[Taking actions](taking-actions.md), and it is a ceiling rather than a pool. There is nothing to
subtract, and no second number to keep.

## Going down

You have stamina in each Skill, starting equal to the sides on that Skill's die. Every blow,
every argument lost, every horror witnessed costs **one point in the Skill that suffered**.

Your ceiling falls as it goes. Roll above your ceiling and the result becomes your ceiling.

**At zero in any one Skill, you are out of the scene.** Not dead. Out.

That last distinction is his, and it comes from a real table:

!!! quote "Jeff, on a Nancy Drew one-shot he ran"

    *"I decided to remove death from the game since we were playing kids... enemies fell
    unconscious in the same way that players do, and after the fight, the players had the
    option of tying them up, hauling them to a safe place or prison, waking them up to question
    them, or just leaving them to recover."*

    His conclusion elsewhere, and the line this chapter is built on: *"Nothing so permanent as
    death, though. The options should range from no effect to temporary disability to permanent
    disability to bleeding out."*

## What being out means

Out is not a coma and it is not a countdown. It is **whatever the fiction says it is**, chosen
by the table from the Skill that broke:

| The Skill that hit zero | What that looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Physical** | Unconscious, winded, a leg that will not hold you |
| **Mental** | Overwhelmed, concussed, unable to hold a thought |
| **Social** | Humiliated, broken, unable to face the room |

You can be carried, tied up, questioned, argued with, or left. You cannot act.

**Nobody dies without the table deciding they do.** Death is available &mdash; some stories need
it &mdash; but it is a choice someone makes out loud, not an arithmetic result.

## Coming back

Stamina returns three ways, and Jeff's own line governs which:

!!! quote "From his notes"

    *"arcane heals arcane, physical heals physical"*

That maps cleanly onto the three Skills, and it is why healing here is not one spell that fixes
everything:

| | Recovers | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A moment's respite** | 1 point in one Skill | Catching your breath, a word from a friend |
| **A night's rest** | All Physical and Mental | Requires food and shelter. His note: *"Food and drink are required to heal"* |
| **Care from someone** | 1 point, any Skill | Medicine for Physical, counsel for Mental, company for Social |

**Social stamina does not come back on its own overnight.** Someone has to give it to you.
That asymmetry is deliberate: a night alone does not fix having been humiliated in front of
people who mattered.

!!! decision "OPEN DECISION — how fast should this be?"

    The recovery rates above are the least defensible thing in this chapter. Jeff's notes
    circle rests repeatedly &mdash; a long thread about how often 5e lets you have one, a house
    rule about long rests only at inns, Mork Borg's *"short rest: heal d4"* &mdash; without
    landing anywhere.

    **This draft is deliberately generous**, because the one page promises a night out rather
    than a war of attrition, and a group that spends the session recovering is not playing.

    **Question for Jeff:** should a night put you fully back on your feet, or should being
    hurt follow you into the next session? That is a tone decision, not a maths one, and it is
    genuinely yours.

## Scars

When something ought to leave a mark, it leaves a mark &mdash; and it is written on the
character sheet rather than tracked as a penalty.

His notes are clear that this should not always be punishing: *"If the situation doesn't call
for a permanent disadvantage, consider something cosmetic like a visible wound, scar, or
burn."* And he was explicit that disability belongs in this game **respectfully and
meaningfully**, not as a stat penalty &mdash; a whole section of `Effects.md` is a list of
conditions he wants represented, alongside sign language, prosthetics, and a combat wheelchair.

So: a scar is part of who someone is now. If the table wants it to carry a mechanical weight,
it lowers a ceiling by one. Most of the time it should simply be true about you.

## What this chapter does not do

**No wound categories, no injury tables, no death saves, no resurrection economy.** All four
appear in his notes as things he was weighing, and each would add more machinery than the rest
of the game contains put together.

Resurrection in particular he had five competing options for and settled none. It is a setting
question more than a rules one &mdash; whether the dead come back at all is something Timaeus
should answer, not this chapter.
