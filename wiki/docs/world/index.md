Does Play Dice is setting-agnostic: the rules don't assume a genre. You can run a heist, a
dinner-party murder, a dungeon, a haunted colony ship. But a game ships better with one setting
in the box, so a new Narrator has somewhere to start.

## What exists in Jeff's notes

**Timaeus** is by a wide margin the largest single body of material in the folder — roughly
**91,000 words across 33 files**: creation story, history, cities, calendar, bestiary, economy,
magic, organizations, crime and punishment, haunted locations, a world map with seven
continents and twenty realms.

It has a genuinely good hook. The world is named for the Plato dialogue that assigns the
Platonic solids to the elements — which means **the dice are in-world cosmology, not just a
mechanic.** The five solids are the five elements. That's a real piece of design and it ties
the system to the setting in a way most games never manage.

The material is early: mostly outlines, fragments, to-do lists, and AI co-writing sessions
rather than finished prose.
