Five dice sit on the table. They are the five Platonic solids &mdash; the only five shapes that
can be built entirely from identical regular faces. In this world they are also the five
elements, and the five spheres that carry the planets.

The dice are not a way of representing the cosmology. They **are** the cosmology.

## Why five, and never six

This is the part that does the quiet work. There are exactly five Platonic solids. Not five that
somebody picked &mdash; five that can exist, proved in Euclid and true for as long as geometry is.

So the set of dice is closed for a reason that has nothing to do with game design. There is no
sixth die because there is no sixth solid. A system that can never sprawl is a rare thing to be
handed for free.

## The elements are Plato's

From the dialogue the world is named after, written around 360 BC. Plato gives four of the solids
to the four elements &mdash; fire to the tetrahedron, earth to the cube, air to the octahedron,
water to the icosahedron &mdash; and the fifth, the dodecahedron, to the cosmos entire.

Jeff's own note opens the creation story with it:

> Timaeus is the dialogue in which Plato proposes that the elements are associated with various
> geometric shapes, what came to be known as the Platonic solids.

## The planets are Kepler's

In *Mysterium Cosmographicum*, 1596, Kepler tried to explain why the planets sit at the distances
they do. His answer was to nest the five solids between the six planetary spheres. Reading
outward from the Sun:

<div class="dpd-kepler" markdown>

Mercury &middot; **octahedron** &middot; Venus &middot; **icosahedron** &middot; Earth &middot;
**dodecahedron** &middot; Mars &middot; **tetrahedron** &middot; Jupiter &middot; **cube**
&middot; Saturn

</div>

The model is wrong, of course. It is also one of the most beautiful wrong ideas anybody has had,
and Kepler thought it was the best thing he ever did.

!!! success "The nod Jeff asked for is already in the mechanic"

    `Creation story.md` carries a note to self: *"The world now needs a nod to Kepler who built
    upon Plato's ideas in Mysterium Cosmographicum."*

    It is already there. The arcane table in Jeff's rules notes assigns a planet to every school,
    and the order is Kepler's nesting exactly &mdash; octahedron to Mercury, icosahedron to Venus,
    dodecahedron to Earth, tetrahedron to Mars, cube to Jupiter. Written down twice, four hundred
    years apart, and they agree.

## The die size is not arbitrary either

A die has as many sides as its solid has faces. The tetrahedron has four, so fire is a d4. The
icosahedron has twenty, so water is a d20. Nothing here was assigned; it was counted.

That is also why bigger dice mean more in this game. Reaching for a d20 is reaching for the most
complicated solid there is.

## Where it reaches the table

The [arcane path](../rules/paths.md) maps a school of magic to each element, which means a
character who works in fire is rolling a tetrahedron because it is fire, not because a table said
so. The [odds page](../rules/odds.md) has what each die does against each other die.
