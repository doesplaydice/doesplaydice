## In plain terms

You may **copy it, change it, translate it, print it, and sell what you make** — including
commercially, and including things you keep closed. You do not need permission and you do not
need to share your work back.

The one requirement is **credit**.

## The attribution to use

If you changed things, say so. That is the whole obligation.

## What is *not* covered

The licence applies to the **text**: the rules, tables, examples and written material in this
repository.

It does **not** grant rights to:

- The name **Does Play Dice**
- The logo or other brand marks

You are free to build on the game and say your work is *compatible with* or *based on* Does
Play Dice. You may not present your work as being the official game, or use the name and marks
as though it were.

That separation is deliberate, and it is the standard arrangement for open projects: the work is
free to fork, the name stays accountable. It is how a stranger can tell the difference between
the game and something that merely resembles it.

## Why CC BY

The Open Gaming Licence turned out to be revocable, and in 2023 an attempt to revoke it nearly
took a twenty-year ecosystem with it.

**CC BY cannot be revoked.** Not by Jeff, not by anyone who acquires this project later, not by
anyone at all. That is the entire point. It is also the licence Wizards of the Coast ultimately
released the D&D 5.1 SRD under, which means material can move between the two.
